\subsection{Comparisons Between Published Methods}
\label{sec:rq1-pub}

\Cref{fig:forest} shows every comparison between two published methods that a family allows, with each subsystem's difference and its CI: \NMainPubPairs pairs, \NReOnePubPairs on RE1, \NPetshopPubPairs on \petshop, and \NOpenrcaPubPairs on \openrca, where the comparison is ours (\cref{sec:design-bench}).
\NMainPubEdiagPairs of them involve \ediag, which is never more accurate than the other method on any subsystem, and none of these reverses.
Of the \NMainPubPairs pairs, \NMainPubRev reverse on at least one subsystem and \NMainPubRevCI do so with CI support, both on one \petshop scenario (on RE1 and \petshop alone, \NAudPubRev and \NAudPubRevCI of \NAudPubPairs).
Which pairs reverse is decided by the two quantities of \cref{sec:design-stats}, the pooled margin and the between-subsystem spread of $\Delta_s$ (\cref{fig:margin}).
Of the \NPubAbove pairs whose margin exceeds the spread, \NPubAboveHold hold on every subsystem; the exception is \circa vs \cfa on \petshop, reversed on the high-traffic scenario by \ExAboveRevHigh \ExAboveRevHighCI.
Of the \NPubBelow pairs whose margin is below the spread, \NPubBelowRev reverse, among them both CI-supported reversals.
With probes and shipped baselines, \NAllBelowRev of the \NAllBelow pairs below the line reverse and \NAllAboveRev of the \NAllAbove above it.
The two tables in which the benchmark papers themselves split a score by stratum, \rcaeval's RE2-TT accuracy by fault type and \petshop's accuracy by scenario and fault type, split the same way: \NTabBelowRev of \NTabBelow pairs with margin below spread reverse on some stratum, against \NTabAboveRev of \NTabAbove above it.
A leaderboard reports the margin; the spread is what it withholds.
Dropping the two \petshop scenarios with 8 cases leaves \NMainPubRevNmin reversing pairs, \NMainPubRevCINmin with CI support, and at a Bonferroni-adjusted level \NBonfRevCI CI-supported reversal remains (\cref{sec:threats}).

\textbf{RCAEval RE1.}
Of \NReOnePubPairs pairs, \NReOnePubRev reverses, without CI support.
\baro leads each of the other four published methods on all three applications, with pooled margins from \ReOneBaroMinMargin to \ReOneBaroMaxMargin.
The one unstable pair is \causalrca vs \rcd: its pooled difference is \ReOneCrRcdPooled \ReOneCrRcdPooledCI, made of \ReOneCrRcdOB on OB, \ReOneCrRcdSS on SS, and \ReOneCrRcdTT on TT; both methods are randomized, and the pooled sign itself changes with the seed (\ReOneCrRcdSeedZero, \ReOneCrRcdSeedOne, \ReOneCrRcdSeedTwo).
This is RE1 under the reference configuration; on the files as shipped it is less stable (\cref{sec:rq2}).

\textbf{OpenRCA.}
Under the wrapper of \cref{sec:design-bench}, the three methods order as \baro (\OpenrcaBaroMean), \rcd (\OpenrcaRcdMean), \ediag (\OpenrcaEdiagMean).
\baro's lead over \rcd is \OpenrcaBaroRcdPooled \OpenrcaBaroRcdPooledCI, but Telecom reverses it (\OpenrcaBaroRcdTel \OpenrcaBaroRcdTelCI); the reversal rests on a few queries, and \OpenrcaBaroRcdSeedsWithRev of the three \rcd seeds show a reversal, on different subsystems.

\textbf{PetShop.}
The published methods are least stable here: \NPetshopPubRev of \NPetshopPubPairs pairs reverse, \NPetshopPubRevCI with CI support.
Both CI-supported reversals come from one cell: \rcd scores \PetshopRcdHighHits of \PetshopHighN on the high-traffic scenario, so \baro (\PetshopBaroRcdHigh \PetshopBaroRcdHighCI) and \cfa (\PetshopCfaRcdHigh \PetshopCfaRcdHighCI) beat it there while trailing it on pooled accuracy, with pooled CIs that include zero.
The two temporal scenarios have 8 cases each; keeping only the two scenarios with at least 25 cases leaves \PetshopPubRevNmin reversing pairs, \PetshopPubRevCINmin with CI support.

\subsection{Same Applications, Second Release}
\label{sec:rq1-release}

RE2 is a second, independently collected release of RE1's three applications, with new fault instances, a sixth fault type, and added logs and traces.
Its metric tables are also sampled differently (\ReTwoRows rows per case against \ReOneSSRows on RE1-SS) and record more metric kinds per service (\ReTwoOBColsMin--\ReTwoOBColsMax columns on OB against \ReOneOBColsMin--\ReOneOBColsMax on RE1).
The four published methods that use metrics only (\baro, \circa, \ediag, \causalrca) run with the same implementations on both releases.
Of the \NCrossPub application-level comparisons available in both releases (\cref{tab:release}), \NCrossPubAgree keep their sign and \NCrossPubChange change it.
\NCrossPubChangeBaro of the \NCrossPubChange involve \baro; the sixth, \causalrca vs \ediag on Sock Shop, moves from \CrossCrEdSSOne \CrossCrEdSSOneCI to \CrossCrEdSSTwo \CrossCrEdSSTwoCI.
\NCrossPubCIopp comparisons have CIs on opposite sides of zero, both on Sock Shop: \baro vs \circa goes from \CrossBaroCircaSSOne \CrossBaroCircaSSOneCI to \CrossBaroCircaSSTwo \CrossBaroCircaSSTwoCI, and \baro vs \causalrca from \CrossBaroCrSSOne to \CrossBaroCrSSTwo \CrossBaroCrSSTwoCI.
Restricting RE2 to RE1's five fault types leaves the count at \NCrossPubAgreeReOneFaults of \NCrossPub.
Within RE2 itself, \NReTwoPubRev of \NReTwoPubPairs published pairs reverse across the three applications (\NReTwoPubRevCI with CI support).

\baro's accuracy falls from \BaroAccReOneOB to \BaroAccReTwoOB on OB and from \BaroAccReOneSS to \BaroAccReTwoSS on SS.
Its ranking is not the input column order, every case has a valid output, and the root cause is still among its first three candidates in \BaroReTwoOBAccThree of the OB cases and \BaroReTwoSSAccThree of the SS cases; what changes is the first position.
RE2's reduced table adds disk I/O and socket metrics for every service, which RE1's tables do not contain, and \baro ranks the disk I/O of the application's database first in \BaroReTwoOBTopMetricN of the 90 OB cases and \BaroReTwoSSTopMetricN of the 90 SS cases, whatever the injected fault.
\citet{fang2025simplerca} report top-1 accuracies of 0.14 on both applications for their own \baro baseline on RE2.
What changed between the releases for this method is the set of metric kinds recorded, a part of the score's provenance that \cref{sec:rq2-summary} takes up.

\subsection{Choosing a Method for an Unseen Subsystem}
\label{sec:rq1-heldout}

A practitioner choosing a method for a new system has only the other subsystems to go by.
Holding out each of the 11 subsystems in turn and ranking the published methods of its family on the remaining ones gives \HNDec adjacent-pair decisions (RE1 \HDecReOne, \openrca \HDecOpenrca, \petshop \HDecPetshop).
In \HErr of them the higher-ranked method is less accurate on the held-out subsystem (\HStrict with a CI excluding zero), and \HTies further decisions are ties.
The \HErr wrong choices fall on pairs that \cref{sec:rq1-pub} places below the line.
On RE1 all \HErrReOne are \rcd vs \causalrca, the pair whose pooled sign changes with the seed; on \openrca the one is \baro vs \rcd on Telecom, the reversal with a CI including zero; on \petshop \HErrPetshopHigh are on the high-traffic scenario, among them \rcd vs \baro, and \HErrPetshopTmpOne on the 8-case temporal scenario.
None of the \HEdiagDec decisions that involve \ediag is wrong.
Computed on the remaining subsystems alone, margin and spread do not single out the wrong choices: \HRecErr of the \HRec decisions whose margin exceeds the spread there are wrong, and \HFlagErr of the \HFlag others (replication package).
\Cref{tab:regret} gives the cost of such a choice as regret.
The pooled winner chosen without the held-out subsystem has positive regret on \LosoRegPubN of the \LosoRegPubOf subsystems (up to \LosoRegPubMax), and with probes and shipped baselines on \LosoRegAllN of \LosoRegAllOf (up to \LosoRegAllMax).
The largest costs are concrete: on RE2, \circa has the highest pooled accuracy (\RegReTwoWinnerPooled) but scores \RegReTwoTTwinner on Train Ticket against \simplerca's \RegReTwoTTbest; on \petshop the pooled winner \circa trails another published method on the high-traffic scenario by \RegPetshopHigh.

\subsection{Probes and Shipped Baselines}
\label{sec:rq1-all}

Adding the three probes and \petshop's shipped baselines gives \NMainAllPairs pairs in the main families, of which \NMainAllRev reverse and \NMainAllRevCI with CI support (RE1 \NReOneAllRev/\NReOneAllRevCI of \NReOneAllPairs; \openrca \NOpenrcaAllRev/\NOpenrcaAllRevCI of \NOpenrcaAllPairs; \petshop \NPetshopAllRev/\NPetshopAllRevCI of \NPetshopAllPairs).
The probes do not win everywhere or lose everywhere: \cdmin is the best method on RE1-SS but ranks below \baro on RE1-TT, and \alertcount leads \openrca's pooled ranking.
On \petshop, the shipped ranked-correlation baseline has the highest pooled accuracy (\PetshopRankedCorrMean), and its pooled advantage over every published method except \circa has a CI that excludes zero.
A leaderboard that lists only published methods therefore ranks methods that a shipped baseline of the same benchmark outperforms.

\subsection{Across Benchmark Families}
\label{sec:rq1-cross}

\XNMethods methods (\baro, \rcd, \ediag, and the three probes) have outputs on all 11 subsystems, so their comparisons can be followed across families by the signs of their subsystem differences (\cref{sec:design-stats}; per-pair tables in the replication package).
Of the \XNPairs pairs, \XNBothSigns have subsystem differences of both signs; the other \XNOneSign all involve \ediag, which is never more accurate than any of the other five on any subsystem, so a ranking that places it last holds everywhere.
Dropping any one family leaves both signs in \XLofoBoth of \XLofoN pair-by-family cases, and dropping any two subsystems in \XLksoBoth of \XLksoN: no order of the other five methods, on whatever scale the families were averaged, holds on every subsystem.
